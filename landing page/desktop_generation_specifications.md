OPESINSURE DESKTOP GENERATION SPECIFICATIONS

1. PRODUCT POSITIONING
- OpesInsure is a digital insurance marketplace, not a single insurer.
- Customers should be able to compare, buy, pay for, manage, renew, and claim insurance through one platform.
- The website must communicate choice, convenience, trust, and licensed-provider access.

2. VISUAL SYSTEM
- White-first interface.
- Primary colors: deep navy / brand blue / restrained gold accents.
- Avoid color collisions and over-saturation.
- Maintain the OpesInsure identity: Africa symbol, subtle heritage/tribal patterns, dotted Africa/network motifs, blue-gold ribbons, and clean professional typography.
- Layout should be simple, professional, clean, industry-standard, and gap-free.

3. GLOBAL UI RULES
- High resolution desktop screens.
- Clear information hierarchy and strong whitespace.
- Sidebars, cards, ribbons, and heritage motifs must stay consistent across screens.
- Do not divert the sidebar branding once established.
- Do not duplicate screens or repeat stages.

4. PUBLIC WEBSITE PAGES
Home / landing page
- Consumer-first insurance marketplace message.
- Search / compare emphasis.
- Dotted Africa maps and interoperability/network cues as subtle support.
- Trust indicators and provider-access message.

About page
- Simple, more narrative and institutional than the landing page.
- Explain who OpesInsure is, why it exists, the marketplace model, Africa/CEMAC focus, technology and trust.
- Keep it professional and not over-colored.

Contact page
- Professional support-oriented design.
- Visible contact channels, support form, offices, support hours, and partnership / media links.
- Refined and not cheap/generic.

5. AUTH PAGES (WEBSITE)
- Separate login and sign-up pages.
- Branded, gap-free, professional, industry standard.
- Use the established identity, heritage patterns, and white-first layout.

6. MARKETPLACE HUB
- This is the broad buy-insurance marketplace, above category directories.
- Include hero search, category navigation, marketplace product cards, filters, comparison entry points, provider trust messages, and side-panels such as compare tray and help me choose.
- Product cards should show provider, product name, key benefits, coverage, starting premium, view-details, get-quote, and compare.

7. CATEGORY DIRECTORIES
- Separate desktop directory page for each insurance line.
- Life, Motor, Business, Health, Travel, Home, Personal Accident.
- Must be gap-free, industry standard, and free from color collision.
- Keep the same structural DNA: hero, category nav, filter rail, product list, support/help panel, and learn-more panel.

8. COMPARE / BUY FLOW
- The many “next stage” screens are sequential desktop stages of the insurance compare-and-buy journey.
- Preserve the established branding and sidebar treatment.
- Each stage should advance the customer toward understanding products, comparing, quoting, reviewing, and buying.

9. DESKTOP ARTEFACTS
- Generate or extract key non-UI visual layers one by one where needed.
- Useful artefacts include: logo lockup, tribal borders, pale African pattern background, dotted Africa maps, blue/gold ribbon systems, glass shield motif, vehicle cutout, scenic hero background, combined hero visual, pale atmospheric blue wash, and gold decorative line accents.
- UI controls, buttons, pagination, filters, and action icons should remain native components, not flattened assets.

10. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
- Use Lucide for UI/action icons.
- Replace sample provider logos with official provider source assets in implementation.
- Rejected screens in the manifest should not be used.
